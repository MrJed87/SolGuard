Based on Open-Meteo model data averaged over the last three full years. In Australia the figures are raised where Open-Meteo reads below ARPANSA's clear-sky model (mostly summer and the tropics); they are never lowered. A month only counts as low when its typical peak is clearly under 3 (below 2.7). Bars show the average of each day's highest UV; the percentage of days reaching UV 3 is in the tap or hover details.

UV figures and burn times are estimates and a guide only, not medical advice. Real exposure varies with cloud, shade, altitude, skin condition, medications and how sunscreen is applied. For official sun protection times see the Bureau of Meteorology and ARPANSA. People of all skin types can be harmed by UV. See your GP about any new or changing mole or skin spot.

Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0), including place search (GeoNames data). In Australia, UV is raised where Open-Meteo reads below the clear-sky UV Index model published by ARPANSA; it is never lowered. Seasonal UV averages use Open-Meteo's historical forecast data.
